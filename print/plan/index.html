1. The idea

UFV SkyGrid is a proposed $100-million autonomous aviation research, education, and regional field-test network designed to connect UFV students and researchers with the Fraser Valley’s aerospace, agriculture, technology, infrastructure, and public-sector ecosystems. Rather than creating only a conventional drone laboratory, SkyGrid would combine a central simulation and innovation capability with regulatory-compliant field-testing locations across the Fraser Valley, allowing autonomous aerial systems to be researched and evaluated under realistic operating conditions. The project would operate around three priorities: Learn, Innovate, and Impact.

The business problem: “UFV has relevant academic disciplines, applied-research ambitions, experiential-learning programs, and access to a region rich in autonomous-system applications, but those assets are not presently organized around a shared platform capable of moving work from classroom learning to simulation, field testing, and industry application.”

Why now: In Abbotsford, agriculture alone includes approximately 1,254 farms operating across 61,586 acres and generating nearly $1 billion in annual farm-gate sales (City of Abbotsford, 2024). Transport Canada introduced a Level 1 Complex pathway for certain lower-risk BVLOS operations beginning November 4, 2025 (Transport Canada, 2026a). SkyGrid uses the Abbotsford aerospace ecosystem mainly as a knowledge, industry, simulation, and systems-integration anchor while locating real-world field testing only where the regulatory environment permits it.

Out of scope

  • A commercial airline or conventional flight school.

  • Weaponized or offensive military drone development.
  • Passenger-carrying autonomous-aircraft services.
  • Unrestricted BVLOS operations outside approved Canadian regulatory pathways.
  • Routine independent emergency-response operations by UFV.
  • Mass manufacturing of certified aircraft.

4. Delivery approach — prove before we spend

Core control principle: SkyGrid does not receive an unconditional $100M spending commitment on Day 1. Each major investment stage must earn permission to proceed. Hybrid, stage-gated delivery: predictive methods for facilities, sites, capital procurement, regulatory deliverables, budgets and milestone dates; adaptive methods for software, analytics, prototypes, research use cases and partner pilots (PMI, n.d.).

The five gates are kill points: each permits Continue, Redirect, or Terminate / Limited-Launch decisions. Example — Gate 3 — Capital investment: continue only if design mature enough for reliable costing; total authorization ≤$100M incl. reserves; site agreements advanced; regulatory path viable; tech specs fit intended missions.

The external deadline remains September 1, 2030; the proposed internal operational-acceptance target is August 15, 2030. The full Gantt chart (Table 10, 24 activities and milestones M1–M7) is attached.

6. Cost and change control

Funding is progressively released through stage gates. Each major WBS branch becomes a control account (PMI, 2026a). If forecast cost exceeds authorization: use valid contingency → value engineer → remove optional Category-C scope → defer enhancements → restructure/recompete procurement → redesign. Do not cut safety, required compliance, cybersecurity, or core student/research capability merely to preserve optional features.
