UFV SkyGrid

Autonomous Aviation Research, Education & Regional Field-Test Network

“Where Autonomous Aviation Meets the Real World.”

UFV SkyGrid is a proposed $100-million autonomous aviation research, education, and regional field-test network designed to connect UFV students and researchers with the Fraser Valley’s aerospace, agriculture, technology, infrastructure, and public-sector ecosystems.

  • Launch: Sep 1, 2030

Three forces are converging on the Fraser Valley — right now.

“UFV has relevant academic disciplines, applied-research ambitions, experiential-learning programs, and access to a region rich in autonomous-system applications, but those assets are not presently organized around a shared platform capable of moving work from classroom learning to simulation, field testing, and industry application.”

“Why not just fly at the airport?”

Side view · altitude slice · not to scale

Lower-risk Level 1 Complex BVLOS operations must remain in uncontrolled airspace, at or below 400 feet AGL, and more than five nautical miles from specified aerodromes; more complex BVLOS operations, including BVLOS in an aerodrome environment, can require a Special Flight Operations Certificate . For that reason, SkyGrid should use the Abbotsford aerospace ecosystem mainly as a knowledge, industry, simulation, and systems-integration anchor while locating real-world field testing only where the regulatory environment permits it.

Our discipline · what we refuse to claim

World’s first drone centre

Do not call SkyGrid the world’s first drone centre or Canada’s first university drone program.

Automatic YXX land & airspace

Do not assume access to YXX land or airspace merely because UFV has an aerospace presence at the airport.

Revenue as the primary pillar

Do not make revenue the primary pillar. Revenue is a supporting sustainability mechanism.

Success = drones purchased

Do not define success by the number of drones purchased. Define it by student learning, research progression, partner outcomes, safe operation, and regional value.

13.2 Definition of Done

SkyGrid is not “done” when construction ends, when drones arrive, or when a website launches. SkyGrid is done when UFV can safely take a real partner or research problem, involve students and faculty, design and simulate an autonomous-aviation approach, obtain the required operating authorization, test it in an appropriate Fraser Valley environment, securely analyze the resulting data, and deliver a meaningful educational, research, or partner outcome within a sustainable operating model.

CH.08 Why Different

Why SkyGrid is different: the configuration, not exclusive technology.

U of T can develop frontier flight-control technology and Foremost can provide specialized restricted test airspace. SkyGrid does not need to recreate either. UFV’s opportunity is to become the place where autonomous aviation meets real Fraser Valley problems, students, researchers, and industries.

BUILD

Capabilities that create UFV differentiation: student-applied model, Challenge Program, interdisciplinary integration, regional workflows, field relationships.

BUY

Mature commercial technologies: aircraft, many sensors, simulation platforms, compute/networking components where appropriate.

PARTNER

Capabilities others already do exceptionally well: specialized test ranges, advanced aerospace expertise, certain industry platforms, regulatory/airport relationships.
